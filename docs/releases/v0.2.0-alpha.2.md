# Transport comparison and iroh connectivity preview

This preview makes the first real transport workloads available in a native
Linux bundle. Download the x86-64 or ARM64 archive and `SHA256SUMS`, verify its
checksum, then extract it. The controller and two standalone example executables
are included; transport evaluation needs no Rust build. Their source is also included.

From the extracted directory, after installing iproute2, nftables, conntrack,
tcpdump and Python 3:

```sh
sudo ./natbench doctor --probe
sudo ./natbench compare examples/transports/direct.json --runs 4 --capture --artifacts ./comparison-001
sudo scripts/check-iroh-connectivity.sh ./natbench ./bin/natbench-iroh-connectivity ./iroh-001
```

- **Matched iroh/Quinn workloads:** fresh endpoints verify identical message and
  bulk bytes behind preserve/random NAT profiles. Reports retain every attempt,
  delivery/failure/unsupported counts, first-data timing, raw application RTT,
  verified bulk goodput, build/settings metadata and diagnostic evidence.
- **Real iroh connectivity:** two NATed peers use a locally configured iroh relay
  and address discovery. Eight cases cover automatic/forced paths, blocked UDP,
  fresh delivery on an established direct connection after relay shutdown, and
  expected application failure when delivery depends on the stopped relay.
  Live cancellation retains partial reports/captures and checks cleanup.
- **Bring your own implementation:** the comparison accepts 2–8 external executable
  adapters through a versioned contract. A custom implementation can stay separate.
- **CI:** bundled application and transport workflow examples pin this preview
  and upload available reports, logs, timelines and bounded packet captures.

The fixture remains Linux/root-only, with fixed IPv4 roles and three kernel
profiles. `compare` measures direct reliable streams to a WAN receiver; the iroh
connectivity scenarios are a separate experiment, not cross-stack relay rankings.
Composable impairments/rebinding, IPv6, broader WebRTC/libp2p/TURN coverage and
portable real-network diagnosis remain future work. Successful synthetic runs and
conditional timings are not production connectivity or performance guarantees.

Input/report versions retain their existing semantics. New comparison and
example formats use independent schema 1 kinds; definitions ship in `schemas/`.
The previous v0.2.0-alpha.1 predates these transport features. Stable v0.2.0 awaits
outside evaluation and resolution of blocking findings.
